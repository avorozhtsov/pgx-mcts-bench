# Exact-budget U1–U5 local study

Date: 2026-07-26

## TL;DR

For this 6×6 Go experiment, **AlphaZero + U3 was the winner**, not
AlphaZero + U1. In the four-agent finalist tournament, AlphaZero-U3 beat:

- AlphaZero-U1 by 40–20;
- MuZero-U3 by 40–20;
- MuZero-U5 by 46–14.

AlphaZero-U1 placed second by beating both MuZero finalists. Therefore
AlphaZero-U3 is the Condorcet winner within the tested finalist set: it
defeated every other finalist directly, with no winner cycle.

Training used three independent seeds, 10 iterations per agent, exactly 256
retained self-play positions per iteration, 32 optimizer steps per iteration,
batch size 32, and 32-channel networks. Search used 32 simulations per move.
Final same-rule AlphaZero–MuZero evaluation used 40 paired-opening games per
seed; same-family and finalist cross-play used 20 paired-opening games per seed.
Every opening was played twice with colors swapped.

The conclusion is specific to this compact training budget, 6×6 Go, the
current network architectures, and the shared untuned exploration constant
`c=1.5`. The complete U1–U5 same-family round robin has not yet been played, so
this identifies the winner among the four selected finalists rather than a
globally established best exploration rule.

## What U1–U5 mean

U1–U5 are **tree-search exploration rules**, not names for the agents. For a
state \(s\) and action \(a\), the search selects an action by maximizing

\[
Q(s,a)+U(s,a).
\]

Write \(N=N(s)\) for the parent-state visit count, \(n=N(s,a)\) for the
action's visit count, \(P=P(s,a)\) for the network prior, and \(c\) for the
exploration constant.

The **AlphaZero agent** searches exact successor states generated by the known
game rules. The **MuZero agent** instead searches successor latent states
generated by its learned dynamics model. This choice of agent is independent
of the exploration bonus: both agents support every rule U1–U5 below.

The benchmark defines:

| Rule | Exploration bonus \(U(s,a)\) | Historical association or role |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | \(cP\sqrt{N}/(1+n)\) | AlphaZero-style PUCT |
| U2 | \(cP\sqrt{N}/\sqrt{1+n}\) | Proposed slow child-count decay |
| U3 | \(c\sqrt{\log(N+1)/(1+n)}\) | Prior-free UCT control |
| U4 | \(c\sqrt{P\log(N+1)/(1+n)}\) | Prior-weighted UCT |
| U5 | \(cP\sqrt{N}\left[c_1+\log((N+c_2+1)/c_2)\right]/(1+n)\) | MuZero-style `pb_c` |

Thus U1 is associated with AlphaZero, but **U1 is not the AlphaZero agent**.
For example, “MuZero with U1” means learned latent dynamics combined with the
AlphaZero-style PUCT bonus, while “AlphaZero with U5” means exact game
transitions combined with MuZero's count-dependent `pb_c` bonus. Keeping the
agent family and exploration rule independent is the central experimental
design of this repository.

The name “AlphaZero-style” is intentional: U1 matches the count-and-prior
dependence normally identified as AlphaZero PUCT, with the tunable multiplier
\(c\). It does not imply that every engineering detail or constant schedule of
every AlphaZero implementation is identical.

## Purpose

This study compares U1–U5 under one equal-data, three-seed protocol. It reports
checkpoint learning curves, same-family rule cross-play, and a paired-color
tournament between the strongest AlphaZero and MuZero candidates.

The main questions were:

1. Does MuZero catch AlphaZero as training progresses?
2. Does an AlphaZero–MuZero match tell us which exploration rule is stronger?
3. Is local GPU acceleration useful enough to justify moving the larger study
   to a Nebius GPU VM?

## Configuration

- Pgx Go 6×6, komi 3.5
- Pass available from ply 24; maximum 72 plies
- Rules-aware MuZero
- U1, U2, U3, U4, and U5
- Seeds 0, 1, and 2
- 32 simulations per move
- 10 training iterations
- Exactly 256 retained self-play positions per agent and iteration
- 32 optimizer steps per iteration, batch size 32
- 32 channels
- 40 paired-opening final arena games per seed
- 10 paired-opening learning-curve games at iterations 1, 3, and 10

Games were completed before their positions were retained, so every retained
position has a valid final outcome. The last retained record may be a prefix;
terminal-transition oversampling only draws from records that actually contain
a terminal transition.

| U2 | 22–18, 27–13, 22–18 | 71–49 | 59.2% | 50.2%–67.5% | 33.4 / 30.7 | 139 / 434 |

The Wilson intervals treat games as independent and are only descriptive:
paired openings and shared trained agents introduce clustering.

U2 showed a severe color interaction. AlphaZero won 14/60 games as Black and
57/60 as White against MuZero. The other rules were much more balanced. This
is further evidence that U2's non-vanishing exploration bonus changes behavior
qualitatively rather than merely slowing U1's decay.

## Learning curves

Aggregate AlphaZero wins out of 30 checkpoint games:

| Rule | Iteration 1 | Iteration 3 | Iteration 10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| U1 | 22 | 20 | 21 |
| U2 | 25 | 20 | 20 |
| U3 | 18 | 15 | 21 |
| U4 | 22 | 25 | 22 |
| U5 | 27 | 21 | 17 |

Across U1–U4 there was no consistent evidence that MuZero is a “stayer” that
overtakes AlphaZero with more training. U5 is different: AlphaZero's aggregate
score fell from 27/30 at iteration 1 to 21/30 at iteration 3 and 17/30 at
iteration 10. This supports the stayer hypothesis and is especially notable
because U5 is MuZero's `pb_c` rule. It does not yet demonstrate an eventual
crossover: the iteration-10 result still slightly favored AlphaZero, and ten
games per seed and checkpoint are too few to establish a smooth learning law.
A 20–40 iteration U5 study is the primary next experiment.

## Same-family cross-play

Each entry aggregates 20 paired-opening games per seed. U1 is the first player
named in the score column.

| Family | Comparison | Aggregate | U1 score | Interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| AlphaZero | U1 vs U2 | 51–9 | 85.0% | U1 clearly stronger |
| AlphaZero | U1 vs U3 | 20–40 | 33.3% | U3 clearly stronger |
| AlphaZero | U1 vs U4 | 36–24 | 60.0% | modest U1 edge |
| AlphaZero | U1 vs U5 | 38–22 | 63.3% | U1 stronger |
| MuZero | U1 vs U2 | 36–24 | 60.0% | modest U1 edge |
| MuZero | U1 vs U3 | 18–42 | 30.0% | U3 clearly stronger |
| MuZero | U1 vs U4 | 26–34 | 43.3% | modest U4 edge |
| MuZero | U1 vs U5 | 18–42 | 30.0% | U5 clearly stronger |

U3 beat U1 in every seed for both agent families. This is the strongest result
of the study. It was hidden by the direct AlphaZero–MuZero comparison: U3 made
MuZero relatively competitive while also improving both families against
their U1 counterparts. U5 exposed an even clearer agent–rule interaction:
relative to U1 it weakened AlphaZero but strengthened MuZero by the same
42–18 margin with which U3 strengthened MuZero. There is therefore no single
rule winner yet; U3 versus U5 and the other missing pairings must be played
before checking for a Condorcet winner or an intransitive cycle.

The formulas still share `c=1.5`, so this is a comparison of the specified
rules, not of separately tuned rule families. A small per-rule `c` calibration
should precede a definitive ranking.

## Cross-family top-two tournament

Based on the incomplete same-family graph, the provisional top two were U3 and
U5 for MuZero and U3 and U1 for AlphaZero. Every MuZero–AlphaZero combination
was played for 20 games per seed. Ten shared openings were each played twice,
with the agents swapping Black and White.

| MuZero agent | AlphaZero agent | MuZero–AlphaZero | MuZero score | MuZero as Black | MuZero as White |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| U3 | U3 | 20–40 | 33.3% | 7/30 | 13/30 |
| U3 | U1 | 23–37 | 38.3% | 12/30 | 11/30 |
| U5 | U3 | 14–46 | 23.3% | 5/30 | 9/30 |
| U5 | U1 | 19–41 | 31.7% | 8/30 | 11/30 |

Together with the same-family results, AlphaZero-U3 is a Condorcet winner
within this four-agent finalist set:

- AlphaZero-U3 beat AlphaZero-U1, 40–20.
- AlphaZero-U3 beat MuZero-U3, 40–20.
- AlphaZero-U3 beat MuZero-U5, 46–14.

AlphaZero-U1 is second within the same set: it lost to AlphaZero-U3 but beat
MuZero-U3 37–23 and MuZero-U5 41–19. Therefore there is no winner cycle among
the four selected finalists. MuZero-U3 versus MuZero-U5 remains unplayed, but
that missing edge cannot change the first- or second-place result because both
lost to both AlphaZero finalists.

This conclusion is limited to the selected agents, current constants, ten
training iterations, and 32 simulations. It does not establish a global
U1–U5 winner because the full same-family round robin and per-rule `c`
calibration remain incomplete.

## Device benchmark and Nebius decision

The MPS path materializes contiguous observation tensors before device
transfer. The device benchmark used an identical one-iteration U1 workload on
the Apple M2 CPU and MPS:

| Agent | CPU | MPS | MPS relative to CPU |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| AlphaZero | 8.7s | 13.8s | 1.6× slower |
| MuZero | 20.1s | 56.8s | 2.8× slower |

The benchmark includes self-play and optimization time recorded by the
trainer. The networks are small and tree/game orchestration remains CPU-side,
so device-transfer and small-kernel overhead dominate.

The predeclared gate for a Nebius GPU run was a meaningful local GPU speedup.
That gate failed. No Nebius GPU VM was created, avoiding cost without losing
useful throughput. Before reconsidering CUDA, the harness should batch across
many more simultaneous roots or seeds so a discrete GPU has enough work.

## Next experiment

1. Complete the U1–U5 round robin within each agent family, especially U3
   versus U5, and explicitly detect three-rule cycles.
2. Calibrate `c` for U1, U3, U4, and U5 on separate tuning seeds.
3. Promote U3 and U5 to the primary alternatives and retain U1 as the standard
   control.
4. Run U5 for 20–40 iterations with five evaluation seeds and larger
   checkpoint arenas to test the MuZero-stayer trajectory.
5. Add a fixed-search evaluation control so learned policy strength can be
   separated from the exploration rule used during arena play.
6. Reconsider a Nebius CUDA benchmark only after actor-level batching is added.
